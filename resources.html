Food and dignity

Why malnutrition still exists in India—and what we can do.

In a country that grows more than enough food to feed its people, millions still go to bed on empty stomachs. But hunger is not always loud. Sometimes, it hides in plain sight.

India does not only face a food shortage. It faces a nutrition crisis.

Malnutrition can appear in the schoolgirl too tired to concentrate, the toddler whose growth is stunted, or the mother who always feeds herself last. A person may eat every day and still lack the nutrients their body needs.

Continue reading ↓

In cities, the problem can look like convenience winning over care: quick snacks in shiny packets, meals that fill but do not nourish, and too little understanding of what growing bodies need. In villages, families may survive on staples because fruit and protein remain difficult to access.

What makes the crisis harder to solve is its invisibility. When people hear “malnutrition,” they often imagine starvation. But it can also mean a child eating only rice each day, a family relying on tea and biscuits, or deficiencies that remain unnoticed until they have already affected health and learning.

Annadatri was born from the belief that food should not simply fill a plate—it should nourish. Our work combines community food support with conversations about balanced meals, anaemia, hygiene and realistic choices using ingredients families already know.

We believe change does not begin with charity alone. It begins with understanding. When young people listen, learn and show up consistently, they can help make good nutrition feel possible rather than distant.

No child should fall behind because they did not receive enough iron. No mother should sacrifice her health in silence. Food is a right, nourishment is a right, and both should be treated with dignity.
